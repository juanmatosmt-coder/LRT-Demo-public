SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT - created for a course demonstration. Not a company procedure.

## Scope
This method images samples in a scanning electron microscope and, when requested, identifies elements with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) on the lab's SEM (SYN-AN-SEM). It answers a stated question about a feature: a contamination, a plating defect, a fracture or a comparison with a good part.

## Required inputs before scheduling
The request must state the question the analysis should answer, and where on the part to look: the feature or area of interest, marked on a drawing or photograph that is attached to the request. It should say whether EDS is needed and which elements matter, whether the lab may cut, section or coat the samples, and whether a good reference part is supplied.

## Sample quantity
There is no minimum. Send the parts that show the feature, and a good reference part when a comparison is needed.

## Sample preparation
Samples are handled with gloves and not cleaned, so that residues are kept. Non-conductive samples may need a thin conductive coating, which can hide light elements; the request says whether coating is allowed.

## Test sequence
1. Photograph the sample as received. 2. Locate the marked area. 3. Image it at increasing magnification. 4. Run EDS spot or area analysis if requested. 5. Repeat on the reference part.

## Reporting
Report the images with scale bars and their locations on the part, the EDS spectra and element results, whether the sample was coated or cut, and the answer to the question asked.
